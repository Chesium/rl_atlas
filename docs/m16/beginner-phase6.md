## Reader tasks and help actually used

These are cross-route reconstructions, not a claim to have attempted all existing exercises blind. Several early station answers were visible during source inspection. For Theorems 4.2 and 4.4, question answers were explicitly stripped from the first read with a read-only regular-expression extraction. The following answers were produced before requesting their answer blocks. Supporting worked examples were available, as the rubric allows. No external mathematical help was needed; the missing route help was the Part 18 prerequisite described in P6-B1.

1. **4.2: reconstruct the dependency chain.** A parameter displacement r first gives joint value/gradient error at most `(L_g + L_grad_g) r`; the residual changes by at most `L_h` times that error uniformly along a trajectory in S. Cover the parameter ball, obtain logarithmic entropy proportional to k, integrate at finite radius, then square the resulting order `sqrt(k/n)` complexity in Lemma 4.1. The zero-residual member removes the reference term only in this later complexity step; it is not a premise of Lemma 4.1 itself. Help used: local reminders in `certificate-classes`, 4.2 Steps 2–5, and `covering-dudley #conventions`. Successfully distinguished the actual residual class from the certificate class.
2. **4.2 questions, answers withheld.** If trajectories can leave all compact sets, Step 3 cannot bound all visited value/gradient pairs by the norm on a single S, and constants may become infinite. A leading term 0.04 at n=1000 becomes `0.04/4 * (log(4000)/log(1000))^3`, about 0.0173, at n=4000; hidden constants remain unchanged, while the logarithm prevents exact quartering. The integral is integrable near zero (`sqrt(log(1/u))`), and the true covering number is one beyond the radius; one must not integrate the loose positive upper bound to infinity. On an infinite horizon converging to zero, the residual approaches zero, so a fixed negative gamma=0.1 cannot hold for every time. For the quadratic features, equality in the value bound occurs at equal absolute coordinates, and a radius-three disk gives `L_grad_g = 3 sqrt(6)`, about 7.348. Help used: the developed proof and quadratic worked case, not answers.
3. **Contraction route: reconstruct a pair bound from a differential one.** For `2 I <= M <= 8 I` and matrix derivative residual `<= -M`, energy rate is 1, distance rate is 1/2, and the convex-region Euclidean pair bound has prefactor 2. Need complete solutions, invariance, and admissible paths; a straight segment is not licensed on an annulus. For the shared nonnormal A and P, the Euclidean energy derivative at `(0.8,0.6)` is +0.16, while metric energy derivative is -1 and metric energy is 1.16. Help used: declared Lyapunov-equation/variational prerequisites and the path-infimum derivation. This task was a reconstruction after source reading, not a blind reuse of the printed exercise.
4. **Matrix route: compare what is actually saved.** Ten monomials mean 55 symmetric entries; `d log^2(d)` is about 53, not 10 as a usable dimension factor. Volume must cover small scales because integrating the Sudakov `1/u` integrand alone diverges. Norm radius is Frobenius, continuity modulus is operator norm. The Lab lacks a fixed upper radius; reading one after fitting chooses the class through the data. Help used: `dual-sudakov` after manually visiting 4.2. The undeclared manual detour is P6-B1. Unknown constants prevent a calibrated sample-size conclusion.
5. **Kernel route, 4.4 answers withheld.** If both approximation errors are `a = gamma/(8 sqrt(2) L_h)`, the stacked error is at most `sqrt(2) a`, so every trajectory residual changes by at most gamma/8. Starting from -gamma yields -7gamma/8; adding gamma/4 yields -5gamma/8, which is at most -gamma/2. A positive learned residual forces the approximation residual above -gamma/8 and the shifted approximation above gamma/8. The shift allows the approximating class's positive shifted violation event to contain the original event while retaining a feasible margin. Halving gamma multiplies the 4.4 leading dependence by 16 through kappa and the outer gamma denominator, versus 4 for 4.2. An independently drawn dictionary can be conditioned on; a dictionary selected after observing failures needs a predeclared family/union bound or fresh validation. Help used: local 4.4 proof, plus the manual 4.2 bridge of P6-B1. Failed to find a sound fixed-gamma premise in the kernel dictionary exercise answer as written: P6-B2.
6. **Figure 1 adaptation and 5.2: separate data and geometry.** `V=x^2+y^2` for the positively damped oscillator gives `Vdot=-2(x^2+1)y^2`; points with y=0 block a strict positive decay rate. In the metric test, horizontal direction `(1,0)` contributes `2 alpha > 0` for M=I at every base state. Zero failures on 100 independent holdout trajectories gives `1-0.05^(1/100)`, about 0.0295, for the checked-time event, not all-time/global contraction. Lowering alpha for a fixed metric can only shrink the matrix violation set; changing alpha in the Lab refits and can change the count either way. Theorem 5.2 assumes true contraction and a uniform state-direction measure, whereas this Lab tests trajectories and full residual matrices at finite states. Printed 5.2 radius is false as stated; its B_H=0 behavior is a counterexample trigger, not permission to claim a global learned metric. The reconstructed theorem keeps a positive floor and remains agent-checked/source-located. Help used: practice, contraction and 5.2 developed explanations. P6-B3 is the one conflicting phrase.
