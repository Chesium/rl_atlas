## Independent calculations prepared before reviewing drafts

These are reviewer recomputations, not proof by experiment. Matrices are dimension two, Euclidean norm unless stated. Numpy checked the displayed matrix products and eigenvalues; the analytic steps below explain the general arguments.

1. Closed loop: `F(x,u)=x+u`, `pi(x)=-2x` gives `f(x)=-x`, flow `phi_t(xi)=exp(-t)xi`, sensitivity `exp(-t)`. For `xdot=x^2`, `phi_t(xi)=xi/(1-t xi)` and `D_xi phi_t=1/(1-t xi)^2`; a positive initial state escapes at `t=1/xi`. Smoothness ensures local uniqueness, not global existence. Sensitivity and uniqueness apply on a common solution interval.
2. Lyapunov chain rule: `V=x^2` along `xdot=-x` gives `Vdot=-2V`; norm decays at rate one. Rotation preserves `x_1^2+x_2^2` and supplies stability without attraction. For strict local decrease, trapping within a compact sublevel set supports continuation; attraction is obtained by excluding a positive limiting value using a negative upper bound on a compact annulus.
3. Shared nonnormal case: `A=[[-1,3],[0,-2]]`, `P=[[1/2,1/2],[1/2,1]]`, `Q=I` gives exactly `A^T P+P A=-I`. Eigenvalues of P are `(3-sqrt(5))/4=0.1909830056...` and `(3+sqrt(5))/4=1.3090169944...`. Thus `Vdot<=-V/lambda_max(P)` and norm rate `1/(2 lambda_max(P))=0.38196601125...`. Eigenvalues of `A+A^T` are `-3+-sqrt(10)`; the larger is positive (`0.1622776602...`), so stable eigenvalues of A do not force Euclidean differential norm to decrease. For `x=(2,1)`, `x^T(A+A^T)x=0`; use a nearby positive direction such as `(3,2)` to obtain derivative `2` rather than claiming this boundary direction grows. `x=(1,1)` also gives derivative zero.
4. Continuous Lyapunov construction: `P=int_0^infty exp(A^T t) Q exp(A t) dt` converges under a Hurwitz exponential bound. Differentiate its integrand, integrate endpoints, obtain `-Q`; positivity follows from the continuous positive integrand at t=0 for each nonzero vector. If `A^T D+D A=0`, `exp(A^T t)D exp(A t)` is constant and tends to zero, hence D=0. This establishes uniqueness without merely counting equations. For diagonal `A=diag(-1,-2)`, `P=diag(1/2,1/4)` when Q=I.
5. Integrating factor: `Vdot<=-lambda V+b` gives `V(t)<=exp(-lambda t)V(0)+(b/lambda)(1-exp(-lambda t))`. For lambda=2,b=1 the limiting energy floor is 1/2. A norm floor requires translating through a positive lower comparison function. Gronwall's `d(t)<=d(0)+L int_0^t d(s) ds` yields `d(t)<=exp(Lt)d(0)`; a nonnegative Lipschitz constant proves only an upper sensitivity bound, not contraction.
6. ISS scalar: variation of constants for `xdot=-x+u` gives `|x(t)|<=exp(-t)|xi|+(1-exp(-t))||u||_[0,t]`, hence the looser ISS bound with gain gamma(r)=r. Its Lyapunov estimate with `V=x^2/2` gives `Vdot<=-x^2/2+u^2/2`. The converse issue is existence of a suitable witness under stated hypotheses; it does not assert that an arbitrary finite candidate class contains one.
7. Discrete Lyapunov construction: for Schur B, `P=sum_{j>=0}(B^j)^T Q B^j` gives `B^T P B-P=-Q` by shifting the sum. Positive definiteness includes the j=0 term; uniqueness follows by iterating `D=(B^k)^T D B^k` and taking k to infinity. For `B=diag(1/2,1/4)`, Q=I, `P=diag(4/3,16/15)` and residual is -I. Forward Euler applied to `xdot=-x` has factor `1-h`, stable iff `0<h<2`; h=2 preserves magnitude and alternates sign. Exact sampling instead has factor `exp(-h)`.
8. Time-shift cost identities: for finite C1 undiscounted `C(x)=int_0^infty ell(phi_t(x))dt`, semigroup splitting gives `C(phi_h(x))=C(x)-int_0^h ell(phi_t(x))dt`, so `grad C f=-ell`. Discounted `C_rho` obeys `C_rho(phi_h(x))=exp(rho h)[C_rho(x)-int_0^h exp(-rho t)ell(phi_t(x))dt]`, so `grad C_rho f=rho C_rho-ell`, whose sign requires checking. Scalar stable example ell=x^2 gives C=x^2/2; discounted unstable `xdot=x`, rho>2 gives `C_rho=x^2/(rho-2)` and positive derivative, despite a finite positive cost.
9. Incremental geometry: in doubled Euclidean state `(x,z)`, distance to diagonal `{(a,a)}` is `||x-z||/sqrt(2)`, attained at `a=(x+z)/2`. Uniformity is over all base positions and pair displacements, not a chosen equilibrium. Triangle inequality through a stable equilibrium produces a bound depending on both initial distances to the equilibrium; it does not by itself yield a bound in `||xi-zeta||`.
10. Variational and metric transport: differentiating the flow gives `Phi_dot=J(phi_t)Phi`, `Phi(0)=I`; subtracting finite trajectories gives a nonlinear remainder, so replacing their separation equation by J delta x is generally inexact. For `f=-x-x^3`, `J=-1-3x^2<=-1`. For energy `E=v^T M(x)v`, product rule is `Edot=v^T[J^T M+M J+sum_i f_i partial_i M]v`. `Edot<=-2 alpha E` implies metric norm decay `exp(-alpha t)` after taking square roots.
11. Path-to-distance: start with every admissible piecewise C1 initial path c(s); transport it as `phi_t(c(s))`. Its tangent satisfies the variational equation; pointwise metric norm decay integrates to length decay. Taking an approximating sequence of initial paths establishes the infimum distance bound without presuming a minimizing geodesic exists. The whole transported path must remain in the certified domain. A lower metric bound yields an endpoint Euclidean lower bound; an upper bound with a straight admissible path yields the simple initial Euclidean upper bound only on convex/whole-space domains (or with an explicit path-length distortion assumption).
12. Deployment: if the expert closed-loop is rho-contractive in a common visited-state metric/norm and plant action dependence is L_u-Lipschitz, uniform action mismatch epsilon on the learner's visited states gives `e_{t+1}<=rho e_t+L_u epsilon`. Unrolling yields `e_t<=rho^t e_0+L_u epsilon(1-rho^t)/(1-rho)`. For rho=1/2, L_u epsilon=.1, e0=0, e1/e2/e3=.1/.15/.175, floor .2. A mean squared error over expert-only states cannot supply the uniform visited-state hypothesis.
